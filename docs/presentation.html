Foundational Principles

Pedagogical Primer: Language vs. Script vs. Tasks

Language vs. Script
Language: Cognitive semantic system (Telugu: Dravidian, SOV).

Script: Visual orthography (Telugu Lipi vs. Latin alphabet).

Tenglish is NOT a new language—it is Telugu transcribed in Latin script!
Translation
Semantic transfer across different languages.

Restructures syntax while preserving propositional meaning.

"నేను కాలేజీకి వెళ్తున్నాను"
→ "I am going to college."
Transliteration & Romanization
Phonetic mapping across scripts without semantic translation.

Romanization: Mapping into Latin characters.

"కాలేజీ" ↔ "college / kaaleeji"
Code-Switching vs. Code-Mixing
Switching: Alternating at clause boundaries: "Call you later. పని ఉంది".

Mixing: Intra-sentential embedding: "naku clg lo exam undi bro".
Text Normalization
Standardizing noisy, colloquial, Romanized orthography into canonical tokens.

"clg ki velthunna bro"
→ "కాలేజీకి వెళ్తున్నాను, bro"
The Tokenizer Failure Mode
Why modern LLMs stumble: Standard Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) shatters "velthunnanu" into 5 arbitrary character shards, exhausting context and losing semantic embeddings.
Historical Trajectory

The 40-Year Evolution of Indian Language MT

1985–1995: Rule-Based MT (RBMT)
TDIL Initiative, Anglabharti, MaTra. Structural transfer via hand-crafted grammar trees and bilingual lexicons.
Why it stalled: Combinatorial rule explosion ($\mathcal{O}(2^n)$); completely brittle on colloquial variations.
1995–2005: Paninian Grammar & Anusaaraka
Akshar Bharati (IIIT-H, Sangal, Chaitanya, Kulkarni). Karaka dependency theory mapped to Telugu case markers (తో, లో, కొరకు).
Why it stalled: Constrained domain coverage; dependency on handcrafted morphological parsers.
2005–2014: Statistical MT (SMT)
IBM Models, Moses Toolkit, GIZA++. Phrase-based probabilistic alignment and n-gram language models.
Why it stalled: Telugu's agglutinative morphology caused severe vocabulary sparsity and OOV errors.
2014–2017: Neural MT (Seq2Seq + Attention)
RNN/LSTM/GRU (Sutskever, Cho), Bahdanau & Luong Attention. Google GNMT (2017) and MS Translator.
Why it stalled: Sequential recurrence prevented horizontal GPU scaling; long-range gradient decay.
2017–2022: Dense Multilingual Transformers
Vaswani et al. (2017). AI4Bharat Samanantar (10.5M pairs), Google Dakshina, Aksharantar, IndicXlit.
Limitation: Subword tokenizers (BPE) trained on formal text fail on noisy Romanized scripts.
2023–2026: Generative LLMs & Multimodal AI
IndicTrans2, Sarvam-1, Meta SeamlessM4T, GPT-4o. Speech-to-speech and multimodal foundation models.
Current Frontier: Context-aware Tenglish code-mixing and real-time on-device inference.

Research Bottleneck

Why Canonical MT Fails on Real-World Tenglish

1. Inconsistent Orthography & Noise
  • No standardized Latin spelling for Telugu words.
  • Canonical "చాలా" is typed as: chala, chaala, tsala, sala.
  • Canonical "వెళ్తున్నాను" typed as: velthunnanu, velthunna, veltonna.
2. Embedded Loanword Morphology
  • English words inflected with Telugu grammatical suffixes:
  • college-ki (college + dative suffix -ki)
  • interview-lo (interview + locative suffix -lo)
  • submit chesava? (submit + past interrogative helper)
3. Subword Tokenizer Shattering
  • BPE tokenizers are trained on formal English and native scripts.
  • "velthunnanu" is fragmented into: [vel, ##th, ##un, ##nan, ##u].
  • Exhausts attention context and destroys semantic embeddings.
4. Blind Transliteration Corruption
  • Transliteration tools blindly convert all Latin text to Telugu:
  • "college" → "కోల్లెగె" (corrupted gibberish).
  • Must have a copy mechanism to preserve English words verbatim.

NormMix AI: Deep Learning Architecture

2-Layer BiGRU Encoder
  • Character-level embeddings ($e(x_i)$) capture fine-grained phonetics.
  • Forward and backward recurrent state aggregation: $$h_i = [\vec{h}_i; \overleftarrow{h}_i] \in \mathbb{R}^{2 d_h}$$
  • Immune to subword tokenization fragmentation.
Scaled Dot-Product Attention
  • Dynamic alignment between decoder state $s_t$ and encoder states $h_i$: $$e_{t,i} = \frac{s_t^T W_a h_i}{\sqrt{2 d_h}}, \quad \alpha_{t,i} = \text{Softmax}(e_{t,i})$$
  • Context vector: $c_t = \sum_i \alpha_{t,i} h_i$.
Pointer-Generator Copy Gate ($p_{\text{gen}}$)
  • Sigmoid copy gate: $p_{\text{gen}} = \sigma(w_c^T c_t + w_s^T s_t + w_x^T e(y_{t-1}) + b)$
  • Final emission: $P(w) = p_{\text{gen}} P_{\text{vocab}}(w) + (1 - p_{\text{gen}}) \sum_{i: x_i = w} \alpha_{t,i}$
  • Result: 100% preservation of English loanwords by direct copying!
OmniProcessor Pipeline
  • Auto-detects input modality (English, Telugu script, Tanglish, Bi-scriptal).
  • Simultaneously generates 4 Target Representations:
    1. Standard Normalized (Telugu script + Preserved English)
    2. All Telugu Script (Transliterated loanwords)
    3. All Romanized Tenglish
    4. English Semantic Gloss & Vocabulary Meaning

Academic Integrity

Objective Comparison: Strengths & Limitations

What Existing Systems Do Well
  • Formal Script Translation: Google Translate & IndicTrans2 are unmatched on legal, literary, and news Telugu script.
  • Speech Synthesis: Meta SeamlessM4T & Sarvam Bulbul excel on acoustic spoken audio.
  • Massive Data Scale: 10M+ parallel sentences.
Where Existing Systems Fail
  • Subword tokenizers fail on informal Romanized Tenglish orthography.
  • Transliteration tools blindly corrupt English loanwords (college → కోల్లెగె).
  • Lack of in situ browser context-menu tools for daily messaging.
What NormMix AI Specifically Solves
  • 100% loanword preservation via Pointer-Generator copy gate.
  • Sub-10ms latency on consumer GPU (3.05M params).
  • Omni-directional multi-modal output (4 simultaneous targets).
  • Seamless in situ browser integration.
What Remains Unresolved (Honest Limits)
  • Deep Generative Prose: NormMix provides semantic glossing, not full multi-clause English prose generation.
  • Dialectal Diversity: Training corpus lacks rural Telangana & Rayalaseema colloquial slang.
Final Synthesis

Conclusion & Future Research Roadmap

Core Research Findings
  1. Telugu MT has successfully evolved across six technological epochs.
  2. Formal Telugu script translation is a solved engineering discipline.
  3. The true frontier is Romanized code-mixed Tenglish.
  4. Copy mechanisms prevent English loanword degradation.
  5. Compact task-specific models enable real-time browser accessibility.
Seventeen Future Directions
  • Conversational context disambiguation (kadu → కాదు vs. కడు).
  • Personalized typing orthography profiling.
  • Dialectal expansion (Telangana, Rayalaseema slang).
  • INT4/GGUF quantization for mobile NPU offline edge inference.
  • Joint Speech-to-Tenglish transcription via IndicConformer.
CENTRAL ACADEMIC CONCLUSION:
The major challenge in Dravidian NLP is no longer simply translating formal Telugu and English. The emerging frontier is understanding, normalizing, and transforming the informal, Romanized, code-mixed language actually spoken and written by 95M+ people in daily digital communication.
